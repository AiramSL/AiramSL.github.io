Research

My research focuses on the ecology of meso- and bathypelagic fishes and other micronekton, and on how ocean conditions shape where they live and how they move. I combine stratified net sampling, ship and moored acoustics, physiology and respiration measurements to link the biology of individual animals with what they do for the whole ocean: feeding predators and transporting carbon to depth through the biological carbon pump.

Life history and functional traits

Age, growth, reproduction, buoyancy and swimbladder properties of lanternfishes and bristlemouths, the most abundant vertebrates on Earth.

Active carbon flux

Respiration, remineralisation and seasonal active flux by migrant and non-migrant micronekton and gelatinous zooplankton.

Acoustics and vertical structure

Interpreting echoes from deep scattering layers and resolving the vertical structure of fish assemblages from the surface to the bathypelagic zone.
